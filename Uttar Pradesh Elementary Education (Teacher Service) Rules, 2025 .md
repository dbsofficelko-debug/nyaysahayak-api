# **Uttar Pradesh Elementary Education (Teacher Service) Rules, 2025**

## **Short Title, Commencement, and Application**

* **Short Title:** These rules shall be called the Uttar Pradesh Basic Education (Teacher Service) Rules, 2025\.  
* **Commencement:** They shall come into force with effect from the date of their publication in the Gazette (08 September 2025).  
* **Application:** These rules shall apply to all teachers appointed to the Basic Schools maintained by the Board and to the local authorities under the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act.

---

## **Definitions**

In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires:

* **"Act"** means the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009\.  
* **"Board"** means the Uttar Pradesh Board of Basic Education constituted under Section 3 of the Basic Education Act, 1972\.  
* **"Teacher"** means a person possessing the prescribed qualifications and appointed for imparting instructions in a school.  
* **"Appointing Authority"** means the District Basic Education Officer (BSA) of the concerned district.

---

## **Recruitment**

### **Rule 5: Sources of Recruitment**

Recruitment to the various categories of posts shall be made as follows:

* **Primary Assistant Teachers:** 100% by direct recruitment through a competitive examination.  
* **Upper Primary Teachers:** 50% by direct recruitment and 50% by promotion from amongst permanent Assistant Teachers of Primary Schools.  
* **Headmasters:** 100% by promotion based on seniority subject to the rejection of the unfit.

### **Rule 8: Academic Qualifications**

* **Basic Qualification:** A Bachelor’s degree from a University established by law in India.  
* **Professional Qualification:** Two-year Diploma in Elementary Education (D.El.Ed.) or equivalent qualification as prescribed by NCTE.  
* **Teacher Eligibility Test (TET):** Mandatory qualification of the State TET or Central TET (CTET) for the relevant level.

---

## **Transfers**

### **Rule 21: General Principles of Transfer**

* **Inter-District Transfer:** Teachers may be transferred from one district to another only once in their entire service period, subject to specific government policy.  
* **Intra-District Transfer:** Annual transfers within the district shall be conducted based on the pupil-teacher ratio (PTR) to ensure balanced staffing.  
* **Mutual Transfer:** Applications for mutual transfers (both inter and intra-district) shall be considered twice an academic year.  
* **Preference Criteria:** Weightage in transfers shall be given to female teachers, differently-abled teachers, and those suffering from serious ailments.

---

## **Disciplinary Actions and Penalties**

### **Rule 24: Suspension and Procedure**

* **Suspension:** A teacher may be placed under suspension by the Appointing Authority where a disciplinary proceeding is contemplated or pending.  
* **Subsistence Allowance:** During suspension, the teacher shall be entitled to a subsistence allowance equal to half of their basic pay.

### **Rule 25: Penalties**

The following penalties may be imposed for misconduct: | Penalty Category | Specific Penalties | | :--- | :--- | | **Minor Penalties** | Censure, withholding of increments (without cumulative effect), or recovery from pay. | | **Major Penalties** | Withholding of increments (with cumulative effect), reduction to a lower post, or removal/dismissal from service. |

Based on the **RTE New GO \- 08 September 2025**, here is the detailed summary of the **Pupil-Teacher Ratio (PTR)** requirements for Basic Schools in Uttar Pradesh:

## **Pupil-Teacher Ratio (PTR) Requirements**

The maintenance of the PTR is a mandatory requirement to ensure balanced staffing and quality education across all basic schools.

### **1\. Primary Schools (Class 1 to 5\)**

For Primary schools, the teacher strength is determined by the number of admitted children:

* **Up to 60 Children:** 2 Teachers.  
* **61 to 90 Children:** 3 Teachers.  
* **91 to 120 Children:** 4 Teachers.  
* **121 to 200 Children:** 5 Teachers.  
* **Above 150 Children:** 5 Teachers plus 1 Headmaster.  
* **Above 200 Children:** The Pupil-Teacher Ratio (excluding the Headmaster) shall not exceed **40:1**.

---

### **2\. Upper Primary Schools (Class 6 to 8\)**

For Upper Primary schools, the staffing is subject to the following minimums:

* **Minimum Teachers:** At least one teacher per class so that there is at least one teacher each for **Science/Mathematics**, **Social Studies**, and **Languages**.  
* **Required Ratio:** At least one teacher for every **35 children**.  
* **Headmaster Requirement:** Where admission of children is above **100**, there shall be a full-time Headmaster.  
* **Part-time Instructors:** In schools with more than 100 students, part-time instructors are provided for **Art Education**, **Health & Physical Education**, and **Work Education**.

---

### **3\. Implementation and Monitoring**

* **Annual Mapping:** BSA (District Basic Education Officers) must conduct an annual mapping of the PTR in every school before the start of the academic session.

## **2\. Headmaster Promotion Criteria**

Promotion to the post of Headmaster is governed by **Rule 18** and **Rule 5** of the 2025 Rules:

### **Eligibility and Process**

* **Source of Recruitment:** 100% of Headmaster posts are filled via promotion.  
* **Minimum Service:** A teacher must have completed at least **five years** of continuous permanent service as an Assistant Teacher to be eligible for promotion to Headmaster of a Primary School.  
* **Selection Principle:** Promotion is based on **Seniority subject to the rejection of the unfit**.  
* **Probation:** Promoted Headmasters must undergo a probation period of **one year**, which can be extended if performance is not satisfactory.

  ### **Rejection of the Unfit**

A teacher may be deemed "unfit" for promotion if they have:

* Received a **Major Penalty** in the last three years.  
* Ongoing disciplinary proceedings for financial embezzlement.  
* Consistently poor results in the **Annual Performance Appraisal (APA)**.  
  ---

  ## **3\. Training Requirements**

Following promotion or recruitment, the following training is mandatory:

* **Induction Training:** All newly promoted Headmasters must undergo a 15-day leadership and administrative training at the **DIET** (District Institute of Education and Training).  
* **ICT Training:** Mandatory certification in basic computer operations is required within the first year of promotion.

### **2\. Procedure for Challenging Transfers**

Teachers may challenge a transfer order if it violates the established principles of the Pupil-Teacher Ratio (PTR) or the weightage point system:

* **Timeframe for Appeal:** An appeal against a transfer order must be filed within **15 days** of the issuance of the order.  
* **Grievance Portal:** All appeals related to inter-district and intra-district transfers must be submitted through the dedicated **Manav Sampada portal**.  
* **Disposal of Appeal:** The Appellate Authority is required to dispose of the appeal with a reasoned order within **30 days** of receipt.  
* **Stay on Transfer:** Filing an appeal does not automatically stay the transfer order; the teacher must join the new post unless a specific stay is granted by the Director of Basic Education.


  ### **3\. Procedure for Challenging Denied Promotion**

If a teacher is overlooked for promotion to Headmaster despite meeting the seniority and eligibility criteria, they may seek redressal:

* **Representation to BSA:** The teacher must first file a formal representation to the BSA requesting a review of the "Unfit" designation.  
* **Formal Appeal:** If the representation is rejected, a formal appeal can be filed with the **Divisional Assistant Director of Education (Basic)**.  
* **Grounds for Appeal:** Valid grounds include errors in the seniority list, incorrect calculation of service years, or procedural lapses in the Selection Committee meeting.  
* **Finality:** The decision of the Divisional Assistant Director is generally final, though further representations can be made to the Board of Basic Education in cases of manifest injustice.


  ### **4\. Legal Recourse (Writ Jurisdiction)**

As established in various judicial precedents regarding the **UP Sainik School** and **Basic Education Board**(which are considered "State" under Article 12), teachers have the right to approach the High Court:

* **Article 226:** If the administrative remedies are exhausted or the actions are deemed arbitrary and violative of **Article 14**, a teacher can file a writ petition in the High Court.  
* **Clean Hands Doctrine:** As seen in the *Udit Yadav* case, the petitioner must approach the Court with "clean hands" and disclose all material facts, or the petition will be dismissed regardless of merit.

In accordance with **Rule 24** of the **Uttar Pradesh Elementary Education (Teacher Service) Rules, 2025**, regular disciplinary inquiries are governed by a strictly defined timeline to ensure justice is not delayed.

## **Standard Timelines for Disciplinary Inquiries**

Once a disciplinary proceeding is initiated against a teacher, the BSA must adhere to the following procedural deadlines:

### **1\. Initiation and Charge Sheet**

* **Issuance of Charge Sheet:** Within **15 days** from the date of the suspension order or the decision to initiate an inquiry.  
* **Teacher's Response:** The teacher must submit a written explanation to the charges within **15 days** of receiving the charge sheet.

  ### **2\. The Inquiry Process**

* **Appointment of Inquiry Officer:** If the explanation is not satisfactory, an Inquiry Officer (usually a senior administrative official) must be appointed within **7 days**.  
* **Completion of Inquiry:** The Inquiry Officer must conclude the hearing, examine witnesses/documents, and submit the final inquiry report within **90 days**.

  ### **3\. Final Decision**

* **Show-Cause Notice:** Based on the inquiry report, if a penalty is proposed, a second show-cause notice is issued to the teacher, who has **15 days** to respond.  
* **Final Order:** The Appointing Authority (BSA) must pass a final reasoned order within **30 days** of receiving the teacher's final response.  
  ---

  ## **Important Legal Safeguards**

As reinforced by judicial precedents involving the **UP Sainik School** and other State institutions, these timelines are not merely suggestions; they are safeguards against administrative overreach:

* **Subsistence Allowance:** During the entire inquiry period, a suspended teacher must be paid **50% of their basic pay** as subsistence allowance, which can be increased to **75%** if the inquiry is delayed beyond six months through no fault of the teacher.  
* **Principles of Natural Justice:** Any failure to provide a personal hearing or access to relevant documents (as seen in the *Dharamvir Singh* and *Dr. Sursari Tarang Misra* cases) makes the final order liable to be quashed by the High Court.  
* **Simpliciter Termination:** For temporary or probationary staff, the school can issue a "Termination Simpliciter" (without assigning specific misconduct) as long as it isn't "stigmatic"—meaning it doesn't imply a permanent blot on the teacher's character without a proper inquiry.
